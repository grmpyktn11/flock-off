What a plate reader does

Photographs everyone

Not just wanted cars. Every plate that passes is read and logged.

Keeps plate, time, place

Enough to reconstruct where a car has been. The records are searchable later.

Shared between agencies

A reader on your street answers questions asked from other departments. Retention rules vary by department.

A speed camera looks for one thing. A plate reader logs everyone and sorts it out later. That is the difference this map draws.

You can route around some of them

Planned by the live pipeline. Dashed gray is the fastest route; solid green is the detour, which Google itself drives from a deep link. Green dots are cameras the detour slips; red ones still see the car.

    Valhalla is here for one reason: it is the only engine that will route around a polygon.

    The numbers are honest

    A trip avoids a reader or two for a couple of minutes. When nothing can be dodged, it says so.

    Does the placement match the need?

    Every camera is scored against the public record. Tap one on the map to see its factors.

    • Crime and arrests DC MPD incidents and arrests; Fairfax PD's weekly report.
    • Income Census tract median against its county's, from the ACS.
    • A score, or nothing Crime and arrests carry it; income alone never produces a number.

    246 of 2,890 cameras have enough published data to score. The rest say so rather than guessing, because thin public records are themselves the finding.
