    How it works

    Pick a destination with Google's own autocomplete. The planner asks Google for the route it would normally drive and looks up every camera whose field of view that route crosses, so you know what sees you before you leave. Valhalla, an open routing engine, treats each field of view as a polygon on the map and proposes a camera-aware alternate; one that does not actually pass fewer readers is thrown away.

    The alternate is pinned with a few waypoints and handed to the Google Maps app, which does the actual driving: turn by turn, lanes, and traffic. Any camera still on the route is called out; the app speaks a heads-up as you approach it, so you always know when you are on camera.

    Does the placement match the need?

    • 50%

      Crime

      Reported incidents near the camera. From DC MPD and Fairfax County PD's weekly report.

    • 30%

      Arrests

      Arrests recorded in the camera's police service area. From DC MPD.

    • 20%

      Income

      Tract median against its county's. From the US Census ACS. Income alone never produces a score.

    246 of 2,890 cameras have enough published data to score. The rest say so rather than guessing, because thin public records are themselves the finding.

    Add your city

    One entry in regions.json. That's it.

  "description": "Northern Virginia, DC and Maryland.",
