    How it works

    Search

    You pick a destination. The backend proxies Google Places, so the phone never holds a key.

    Plan

    It asks Google for the route you would otherwise drive, finds which cameras watch it, and asks Valhalla for one that stays out of their dead zones.

    Hand off

    The detour becomes Google Maps waypoints, checked so Google will actually follow them, and you get a deep link. Google does the driving.

    Warn

    While you drive, the app watches from the background and speaks up about any camera it could not route around.

    Valhalla exists in this stack for exactly one reason: it is the only engine that will route around a polygon. Everything the driver sees is Google's, because Google is what does the driving.

    The numbers are honest

    A Fairfax trip avoids one or two readers at a cost of a couple of minutes. A trip whose cameras cannot be dodged says so, rather than claiming otherwise. Five readers ring George Mason's campus; no route through it avoids them, and the app tells you that.

    Add your city

    Every region the project serves is one entry in regions.json. The camera ingestion and the routing tiles both read it, so the geography is written once.

    Camera coverage comes from OpenStreetMap, where it is mapped by people. If your area looks thin, the most useful contribution is not code: it is mapping the cameras into OSM, where this project and everyone else's picks them up. 

    "dmv": {
  "description": "Northern Virginia, DC and Maryland.",
  "bbox": [37.85, -79.5, 39.75, -75.05],
  "osm_extracts": [
    ".../district-of-columbia-latest.osm.pbf",
    ".../maryland-latest.osm.pbf",
    ".../virginia-latest.osm.pbf"
  ]
}
